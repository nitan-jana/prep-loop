# Readiness

Whether the preparation is working, and when to stop preparing and start
applying.

## Readiness is evidence, not a feeling

Confidence and readiness move independently, and confidence moves first. A
topic recently studied feels ready and is not; a topic drilled weeks ago feels
stale and holds.

So readiness is read from `instance/performance/` — actual grades on actual rounds —
and never from asking the user whether they feel prepared.

## It is per round type

There is no single readiness number. A candidate can be ready for one kind of
round and not another, and averaging the two hides exactly the gap that will
decide the interview.

Every round type carries its own state, reviewed at the review session and
recorded in that week's file.

## The ladder

Four rungs, per round type. Each one is a strictly harder test than the last,
and progress is not assumed to be monotonic.

1. **Can reconstruct** — produces the mechanism when asked, given time.
2. **Can reconstruct under time** — inside the window a real round allows.
3. **Can defend** — the substance arrives in the answer itself, and then
   survives follow-ups on the cost and the rejected alternative.
4. **Can do it cold** — on material not recently studied, drawn from the wider
   pool rather than from the week's work.

A round type reaches a rung when it has held there across **two consecutive
reviews** with no `not retained` in that type. One review is a sample of one;
two is the cheapest thing that is not.

**A single review records an observation, not a rung.** The first review of a
round type has nothing to hold against, so what it writes down is where the
evidence points, explicitly provisional. Reading it as a rung means the second
review computes two consecutive against a number that was never earned.

## Extraction is not defence

An answer that contained everything, once each piece had been asked for, has not
reached the third rung. The follow-ups produced the content rather than testing
it, and an interviewer who does not push hears only the opening.

This is where the ladder and the rubric have to agree. `solid` requires the
answer unprompted, per [`grading.md`](grading.md#the-grades). A rung that
counted extracted content as defence would advance on evidence the rubric had
already refused, and the two instruments would then disagree about the same
round while both looked authoritative.

It is easy to miss because a round of good answers to good follow-ups looks
like a strong round from either side of it. What that round measured is partly
how well the questions were written, and the interviewer who asks fewer of them
gets a different answer to the same question.

Those defaults hold unless `instance/profile/` overrides them, which a user targeting a
different bar reasonably might.

## Regression

Dropping a rung is normal and is treated as information, not as a failure.

Regression is **more urgent than an uncovered topic**, because a rung already
reached is a claim already being made — the resume says it, the story bank
assumes it, and the user is walking into rounds expecting it to hold. See
[`mock-sourcing.md`](mock-sourcing.md#choosing-the-round-type), which ranks a
slipped type above an untouched one for exactly this reason.

The response is to schedule drills on the specific answers that slipped, named
from the review file. Not to re-read the topic from the start, which is the
instinct and which spends a week re-learning what was already there.

## When readiness says go

**The answer to reaching the top rung is to apply, not to prepare further.**

Preparation is unbounded and there is always another topic. The system exists
to produce a defensible signal about when more of it stops paying, and once
that signal arrives, ignoring it is the most expensive thing the user can do
with a week.

The outbound cap in [`caps.md`](caps.md#the-outbound-cap) still holds — readiness
raises the priority of the daily action, never the number of them.

## What it is not

Not a score, not a percentage, and not something to report to anyone. It is a
scheduling input: what to drill, what to leave alone, and whether to spend the
week's marginal hour on practice or on applications.
