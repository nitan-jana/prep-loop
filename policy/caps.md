# Caps

Two ceilings on what a single day may be asked to carry. The numeric values are
in `instance/profile/schedule.md`; the mechanism is here.

## A cap binds the plan, not the person

A cap is a constraint on what may be **scheduled**. The planning session may
not put more than the cap into a day. That is the whole enforcement.

A user who does more than the cap on their own initiative has done more work,
which is fine and gets logged as it happened. A plan that schedules over the
cap is a planning bug, because it produces a week that gets abandoned in the
middle and then reads as a week of failures.

Nothing in the system polices the user against a cap. Nothing marks a day as
over-cap after the fact.

## The content cap

A ceiling on **new material** taken in per day — reading, a lecture, a written
explanation of something not seen before.

Counted **across the whole day, not per block.** Per-block accounting is how a
day ends up with one large item in each of several blocks and no capacity left
to practise any of it.

**Problems are practice, not content.** Solving, drilling, redoing something
already covered, writing a story, running a round — none of it counts against
the content cap. The cap exists because intake without practice produces
recognition rather than recall, and recognition does not survive an interview.

Items are weighted rather than counted flat: a long chapter is not one unit the
way a short article is. `instance/profile/schedule.md` states the ceiling and the
weights it uses.

**Overflow is not dropped.** What does not fit goes to the `## Deferred` list at
the bottom of the week plan and is scheduled first next time. See
[`cadence.md`](cadence.md#carrying-work-forward).

## The outbound cap

A ceiling on **job-search actions** per day, so that a burst of applications
does not replace a week of preparation, and so that each action gets enough
attention to be worth sending.

**One action means one, whatever form it takes.** A cold approach, a referral
request and an application through a board are the same kind of thing and share
the same budget. Sending three in a day is three against the cap, not one of
each.

**These do not count**: following up on something already sent, researching a
company, updating a resume, or replying to an inbound message. None of them is
the act the cap is protecting against.

That is the ceiling and all of it. What an action is, what it leaves behind, and
what has to be true before it goes are in [`outbound.md`](outbound.md), which is
also where the reason the scarcity pays off is written.
