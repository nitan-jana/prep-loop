# Outbound

What a job-search action is, what it leaves behind, and what has to be true
before it goes.

The ceiling on how many happen in a day is in
[`caps.md`](caps.md#the-outbound-cap). This file is the mechanism, and the two
are separate on purpose: a limit answers how often, and nothing in a limit says
what a good one looks like.

## The action is a prep artifact leaving the building

Everything else in the system produces evidence that stays put. This is the one
session whose output goes to someone who did not ask for it, cannot be recalled,
and will be read once.

That is what the scarcity is for. One action a day is worth its scarcity only if
the thing attached to it was chosen for the recipient, which is a decision that
takes the time the cap frees up.

## An ask is not an application

They share the cap and nothing else. Both consume the day's one action, because
attention is the scarce resource and a careful referral request costs what a
careful application costs.

The **record** keeps them apart:

- **An ask** is a request aimed at a person. It has no stage, because nothing
  has been submitted. Most go unanswered, and that is the channel behaving
  normally rather than a rebuff.
- **An application** is a submission aimed at a role. It has a stage, and it can
  move.

A message that carried a resume is **both**, since something was actually
submitted. Whether the resume went with it is the question that decides it.

Mixing the two makes the record unreadable in the direction that matters: a
column of asks and applications counted together cannot answer whether the asks
are producing anything, which is the only reason to keep the column.

## Stages

Four, and deliberately fewer than a job tracker keeps.

1. **Sent** — it went out.
2. **Replied** — a person responded. An automated acknowledgement is not a
   person.
3. **In process** — something is scheduled, or an assessment is outstanding.
4. **Closed** — it ended, whichever way.

**A close keeps the stage it reached.** Nothing moves backward and nothing is
deleted. An application that reached `in process` and ended there is a different
fact from one that never got a reply, and collapsing them loses the more useful
of the two.

The stages exist for one question: **is a round coming, and what kind.** That is
the input the planner needs, and it is the whole reason this system tracks
anything about outbound at all.

## Lead with a claim that survives the source being opened

The artifact attached to an action carries a claim, and it goes out under the
same rule as everything else the user asserts. See
[`claims.md`](claims.md#every-claim-carries-a-marker).

**`verified`, or `stated` with its obstacle named.** A number nobody checked,
sent to a stranger who may open the repository while reading, is the one place
in this system where an unchecked claim reaches an audience before anyone
notices. Every other artifact is read by the user first.

A claim about a public dependency is the sharpest version of this, because it
can be contradicted rather than merely doubted. See
[`claims.md`](claims.md#unverifiable-is-not-unverified).

**Never warm a message with something untrue.** Not an invented mutual
connection, not a shared institution, not a proof point stretched to fit the
role. If the warm version is not true, the colder honest one is what gets sent.

## The week is graded on what was sent

A week in which the actions went out and nothing came back **was a good week.**

Replies measure a market. Actions measure the person, and only one of those is
available to work on. This is the same reason readiness is read from grades on
rounds rather than from outcomes, per
[`readiness.md`](readiness.md#readiness-is-evidence-not-a-feeling); outbound is
the place the principle is easiest to abandon, because the silence is personal
in a way a bad grade is not.

So a shortfall is a shortfall of actions. It is never a shortfall of replies,
and the record is not written in a way that lets it read as one.

## The channel record is a record, not a verdict

Which route produced which reply is worth writing down, and worth almost nothing
to reason from at the volumes one person generates.

A route with few sends and no replies is indistinguishable from a route working
exactly as it should. **Concluding that a channel does not work needs a sample
this system does not try to reach**, and the honest reading of a thin column is
that it is thin.

So the record stays a record. Nothing computes a rate from it, nothing names a
bottleneck, and nothing reallocates effort on the strength of it. A user who
wants that analysis needs volume and a tool built for it, and that is a
reasonable thing to want and not this system's job.

## What readiness changes

Reaching the top rung means the marginal hour goes to applications rather than
to more preparation, per [`readiness.md`](readiness.md#when-readiness-says-go).

It raises the **priority** of the daily action, never the number. The cap holds
regardless of how ready the evidence says the user is, because the cap was never
about readiness.
