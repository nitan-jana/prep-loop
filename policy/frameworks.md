# Frameworks

A framework is scaffolding for producing an answer under pressure. It is not
content, and reciting it is not answering.

Which frameworks a user works with is a profile fact, recorded in `profile/`.
Different targets and different stacks call for different ones, and prescribing
a named framework here would make the system wrong for most people who pick it
up. This file says how any of them is used.

## What a framework is for

**Preventing omission.** Under time pressure the parts of an answer that get
dropped are not random — the same ones go every time, and they are usually the
constraints, the cost, and the alternative. A framework is a checklist that
survives the pressure.

That is the whole job. It does not make an answer correct, it does not supply
the content, and following one perfectly with nothing inside it produces a
well-organised non-answer that grades no better than a disorganised one.

## Announce once, then disappear

Say the shape once, at the start: what the answer is going to cover, in order.
Then follow it without narrating it.

**A visible framework reads as rehearsal.** An answer that keeps labelling its
own steps tells the interviewer that the structure is the thing being
remembered, which invites them to step outside it and see what is left. A
framework is drilled until it is invisible, and until then, drilling it is the
work.

## Every framework has an exit

The step where the answer stops exploring and commits to a choice.

This is the step most often skipped, because exploration feels productive and
committing can be wrong. An answer that surveys options and never picks one has
not answered, and grades as `shaky` at best — see
[`grading.md`](grading.md#the-grades), where naming the tradeoff is what
separates the top grade, and a tradeoff cannot be named without a choice to
attach it to.

When drilling a framework, drill the exit hardest.

## The round grades the answer, not the adherence

A round never marks a candidate down for departing from a framework, and never
up for following one. The rubric in [`grading.md`](grading.md) has no term for
structure.

Where a framework helps is upstream: an answer built with one is more likely to
arrive unprompted with its cost attached, which is what actually earns the
grade. If it is not producing that, the framework is not working and swapping
it is a reasonable move.

**Never coach the framework during a round.** Not "try walking through the
requirements first". That is coaching, it belongs in the review per
[`interviewing.md`](interviewing.md#what-the-review-is-for), and doing it
mid-round destroys the sample.
