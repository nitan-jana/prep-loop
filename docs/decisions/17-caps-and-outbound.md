# A cap binds the plan, not the person

Two ceilings on what a single day may be asked to carry: new material, and
job-search actions. A cap constrains what may be **scheduled**, and that is the
whole enforcement.

## Why nothing polices the user

A user who does more than the cap on their own initiative has done more work,
which is fine and gets logged as it happened. Nothing marks a day as over-cap
after the fact.

A **plan** that schedules over the cap is a planning bug, because it produces a
week that gets abandoned in the middle and then reads as a week of failures.
That is the thing being prevented — not effort.

Two details fall out of it:

**Content is counted across the whole day, not per block.** Per-block accounting
is how a day ends up with one large item in each of several blocks and no
capacity left to practise any of it.

**Problems are practice, not content.** Solving, drilling, redoing something
covered, writing a story, running a round — none of it spends the allowance. The
cap exists because intake without practice produces recognition rather than
recall, and recognition does not survive an interview.

**Overflow is deferred, never dropped**, and scheduled first next time.

## The outbound cap got its own mechanism

The cap file originally carried both the ceiling and a sentence about leading
with the right artifact. That sentence was the visible edge of a whole
mechanism, and it moved out into a file of its own.

The split is stated where it happens: **a limit answers how often, and nothing
in a limit says what a good one looks like.**

## What the outbound file added

**The action is a prep artifact leaving the building.** Everything else in the
system produces evidence that stays put. This is the one session whose output
goes to someone who did not ask for it, cannot be recalled, and will be read
once — which is what the scarcity is for.

**An ask is not an application.** They share the cap, because attention is the
scarce resource and a careful referral request costs what a careful application
costs. The record keeps them apart, because a column counting both together
cannot answer whether the asks are producing anything, which is the only reason
to keep the column.

**Four stages, fewer than a job tracker keeps**, and a close keeps the stage it
reached. They exist for one question: is a round coming, and what kind.

**The week is graded on what was sent.** A week in which the actions went out
and nothing came back was a good week. Replies measure a market; actions measure
the person, and only one of those is available to work on. Outbound is where
that principle is easiest to abandon, because the silence is personal in a way a
bad grade is not.

**The channel record is a record, not a verdict.** A route with few sends and no
replies is indistinguishable from a route working exactly as it should.
Concluding otherwise needs a sample one person does not generate, so nothing
computes a rate from it, names a bottleneck, or reallocates effort on the
strength of it.
