# Three grades, and the failed test named beside them

`solid`, `shaky`, `not retained`. Defined by what the answer did rather than by
how it felt, and the top grade is **conjunctive**: unprompted, plus the cost
named, plus one follow-up survived.

## Why the conditions are listed

A grader cannot drift soft against a definition that lists its conditions. A
scale defined by feel drifts by exactly the amount the grader wants it to, and
nothing on the page records that it moved.

Two supporting rules protect the same thing:

**A grade without the answer is not a grade.** Every grade carries the quoted
answer that produced it, in the user's own words, in the same file. Drift is
only detectable if the evidence is on the page — a file of grades with no
answers cannot be re-read later to check whether `solid` used to mean something
stricter. It is also the only defence against a grade the user disagrees with.

**The anchors start empty and stay empty** until there are real graded answers
to put in them. Onboarding does not invent them: an invented anchor is a grader
calibrated against a fiction, which is worse than an uncalibrated one because it
feels grounded.

## The re-grade audit grades the grader

Periodically, pull graded answers from several weeks back, re-read **only the
answer text**, grade blind, and compare. Coming out consistently harsher than
the original grades means the grader has drifted soft.

Nothing about the result changes the user's standing, and it is not a signal to
work harder. It is the one instrument in the system pointed at the system.

## Resolution added without a fourth grade

`solid` being conjunctive means anything short of it collapses three distinct
failures into one value. An answer that arrived whole and named no cost, and an
answer assembled entirely out of follow-ups, are the same grade and different
problems.

So every grade below `solid` now records **which of the three tests it failed**.
It costs a word and buys the resolution the scale does not have, and it makes
movement legible while the grade holds still: a repeated `shaky` failing fewer
tests than it used to has improved, and without the leg recorded there is
nothing on the page to say so.

**It deliberately does not add a fourth grade.** The pressure on a grading scale
is always toward more room at the top, and a scale with somewhere flattering to
put a disappointing answer will find one.

## What a grade is not

Of one answer, to one question, at one moment. Not a statement about the user's
level, not a trend on its own, and not softened because the week was hard.
Encouragement goes somewhere other than the grade line — "shaky, but much better
than last time" is two claims, one of which is not a grade, and the pair is what
drift looks like from the inside.
