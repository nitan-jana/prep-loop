# Readiness is evidence, not a feeling

Read from actual grades on actual rounds, and never from asking the user whether
they feel prepared.

## Why the feeling is the wrong instrument

Confidence and readiness move independently, and confidence moves first. A topic
recently studied feels ready and is not; a topic drilled weeks ago feels stale
and holds.

## It is per round type

There is no single readiness number. A candidate can be ready for one kind of
round and not another, and averaging the two hides exactly the gap that will
decide the interview.

## Four rungs, and two reviews to earn one

Can reconstruct; can reconstruct under time; can defend; can do it cold. A round
type reaches a rung when it has held there across **two consecutive reviews**
with no `not retained` — one review is a sample of one, and two is the cheapest
thing that is not.

That created a gap worth naming: **a single review records an observation, not a
rung.** The first review of a round type has nothing to hold against, so what it
writes is explicitly provisional. Reading it as a rung means the second review
computes two consecutive against a number that was never earned.

## Extraction is not defence

An answer that contained everything, once each piece had been asked for, has not
reached the third rung. The follow-ups produced the content rather than testing
it, and an interviewer who does not push hears only the opening.

This is where the ladder and the rubric have to agree. `solid` requires the
answer unprompted, so a rung counting extracted content as defence would advance
on evidence the rubric had already refused — and the two instruments would then
disagree about the same round while both looked authoritative.

It is easy to miss because a round of good answers to good follow-ups looks
strong from either side of it. What that round partly measured is how well the
questions were written.

## Regression outranks absence

Dropping a rung is normal and is treated as information. It is **more urgent
than an uncovered topic**, because a rung already reached is a claim already
being made — the resume says it, the story bank assumes it, and the user is
walking into rounds expecting it to hold.

The response is to drill the specific answers that slipped, named from the
review file. Not to re-read the topic from the start, which is the instinct and
which spends a week re-learning what was already there.

## When it says go

**The answer to reaching the top rung is to apply, not to prepare further.**
Preparation is unbounded and there is always another topic; the system exists to
produce a defensible signal about when more of it stops paying, and ignoring
that signal is the most expensive thing the user can do with a week.

It raises the priority of the daily outbound action, never the number of them.

## The drill list is ordered

Because the planner schedules from the top. An unordered list hands the ranking
decision to whoever reads it next, and that decision belongs in the review,
where the evidence is.
